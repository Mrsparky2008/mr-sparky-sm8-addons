The money side, settled

On Friday 14 August three things change at once: Form 001 stops being filled in, material stops being Mr Sparky's cost and becomes the tech's, and commission moves onto the ladder. Everything below is what we agreed. Nothing has been built yet.

01

How a job earns money

after 14 Aug
  • Trigger: job marked Completed in ServiceM8. No form, no gate, nothing else to wait for.
  • Owner: completion_actioned_by. That one field decides the money, the conversion credit, the ladder movement, and who wears a defect.
material = Σ receipts logged to this job + declared van stock
gross = invoice inc GST − material
share = gross × rung rate (minus 2 points if compliance failed)
payable = share + material ← reimbursed, because the tech funded it
  • Claim submitted → every figure freezes. Corrections go forward as adjustments, never by editing history.
  • Adjustments and retention settle → approve → RCTI → paid.
  • GST is on the full value of the supply, not the net of retention.

  • No movement until 10 claims. That is “the first ten jobs establish your rate”.
  • 90-day rolling window, floored at 14 August — nobody arrives already qualified on history.
  • Promotion is immediate. A drop needs two weak reviews running.
  • No claims at all in the window slips a rung — nobody camps on 80% doing a job a fortnight.
  • Three outcomes per assessment: hold at 50 · up to 60 · down to 48 on compliance.
  • The rate locks when the lead is claimed, so a rung change can never move a job already taken on.
Horizon

The ladder is version one

Commission is performance-based for now; a more sophisticated system takes over once the network grows. The plumbing already allows it: rules are effective-dated per contractor and claims freeze their figures, so a future scheme is a new rule version from a chosen date — nothing historical moves, no migration.

Build note

Store the rung and the compliance flag — never store “48%”

A raw 48 loses which rung they are on. Record rung 0, compliance failed and both answers fall out: compliance recovers → back to 50; they climb a rung while still failing → 58. The penalty is always minus 2 from whatever rung they hold, computed fresh.

03

Who owns a job

one field, no sync
  • Tech accepts in Telegram → ServiceM8 assigns it → only they can see it.
  • A tech cannot hand his own job on. Only the office can reallocate. Every handover crosses your desk by construction.
  • Office moves it → A loses access, B gains it → B completes it → B's UUID lands on the job.
  • Nobody records the handover, because the handover is what caused the field to change.
  • The Telegram claim record is never edited. A-claimed-B-finished stays visible as the drill-down if the two ever diverge suspiciously.
  • Conversion: a lead someone else completed leaves A's denominator entirely — not won, not lost. B gets it in both halves of his.
  • Quoted-by / completed-by split is retired. One rate, one person.
  • Match on the ServiceM8 staff UUID, not the name. Aliases exist today only because the Form 001 dropdown drifted; a UUID never does.
04

Material

no office data entry, anywhere
  • Tech photographs the docket → OCR reads it → ABN identifies the supplier → dedupe engine checks it → stored.
  • The sum of those records is the material figure. The database does what the Job_Cost sheet used to do, without anyone typing.
  • Voided receipts excluded. Credit notes carry a negative amount, so a return reduces the total correctly.
  • One docket, one job. No duplicates, no reissuing between techs.
  • Van stock: $50 inc GST per job with no receipt. Per-contractor override; zero is valid for anyone not yet trusted.
  • Flag, not a gate: declared over 5% of job value lands on the approval panel beside the existing receipt-variance flag.
  • Receipt arriving after a claim is submitted → credit adjustment on the next claim, itemised. A standalone materials-only RCTI only if someone actually asks.
Why $50 on trust is safe

A padded declaration is worth cap × (100 − rate)

The material is subtracted from profit before the split and only then handed back. A fake $50 at the rung-0 rate nets the tech $25, not $50 — and $10 at 80%. Note which way that runs: the lower the rung, the more it is worth to them. Least track record, most to gain — which is the argument for starting new people at zero and raising the cap as they earn a rung.

Why not 5% as the cap

Van stock has no receipt and never can

He bought that box of RCDs in June; there is no docket for one of them. On a $250 callout swapping a $30 RCD, a 5% cap is $12.50 — he is over it and cannot comply. He also cannot know 5% at the moment he declares, because the invoice may not be priced yet. Hence a flat cap as the rule, and the percentage as the review flag.

05

Compliance

2 points of the rung
  • ≥80% of jobs with every task ticked → keep the rung rate. Below → rung minus 2.
  • Same 90-day window as conversion, assessed at the same moment.
  • No penalty during the qualifying period — you cannot measure a window that has not happened.
  • Start with the JSA only. Widen once it is boring.
  • Ticking a task writes a diary entry: what, when, and by whom. Better than a yes/no — a JSA ticked at knock-off is not a JSA, and only the timestamp shows that.
  • A rate drop must arrive with its reason attached: “48% — compliance ran at 72%, needed 80%.” Unexplained is a dispute; explained is a conversation about paperwork.
The hole — catch this before building

0 of 0 tasks reads as 100% compliant

You can only police against a known required set. If “required” means whatever happens to be on the job card, then a job with no tasks scores perfect and the worst case wins. The checklist must auto-attach at job creation from a template or category, so the required set is defined by the template, not by the person being measured. Same for the JSA.

06

Warranty

zero build — settled 8 Aug, late
  • Mr Sparky does all warranty repairs. Full stop. Nobody on the network touches them, so there is no rectifier to pay and no two-entry resolution to compute.
  • Raised in ServiceM8 under category 010 — Warranty Claim, handled in-house.
  • The original contractor is charged as agreed in their contract — $85/hr, 3-hour minimum, plus material, or whatever their signed terms say. The office works out the number.
  • The charge lands as a manual back-charge adjustment with the value typed in and the reason spelled out: “Warranty claim job 167*** — $378.00 + GST.” That button already exists in the portal and shows line-by-line at settlement — never a silent deduction.
  • 12 months from completion of the original job — a human check now, not a coded one.
  • The per-contractor warranty rate is a number the office reads from settings, not an engine input. Show it beside the adjustment form as it stood on the original job's date, so the right version gets charged without anyone remembering which amendment applied.
  • warranty.mjs stays in the codebase, wired to nothing — dead, not deleted, same as the Form 001 readers.
Deductions list

Legitimate deductions, named in settings

So the contract can point at them: warranty claim (value entered by the office) and wasted lead — $200 (parked, below). Recovery order stays as the code has it: a charge draws on retention first, so it hits the security rather than that week's pay, and the settlement shows the draw.

Parked — analytics phase

Wasted lead: $200, detect-and-propose

Defined: accepted but never quoted (no quote sent via SM8), or quoted with no follow-up from the SMS/email template in the diary. Both detectable from data already read. The system detects and proposes; the office approves the clip. Built when tech profiling starts, not before.

07

Retention

  • 10% of every claim until the balance reaches $1,000.
  • Whole balance held. Released 12 months after the later of: access to accept jobs removed, or last job completed.
  • Nothing matures while they are active. Rebuilds after a claim draws it down.
  • Negative balance is an accepted business risk.
  • Currently target: 0 in settings, so nothing is withheld today. Setting it to $1,000 turns it on.
08

Settings are the contract

the biggest new idea
  • The negotiated deal is the settings record: rung start, warranty rate and minimum, retention, van stock cap.
  • Admin uploads the signed document into settings → that action locks the terms and timestamps them.
  • The lock records which settings version was signed, by whom, when. Settings are already versioned on every write, so this is nearly free — and it means “what exactly did Peter sign?” is answerable by loading that version, not by inferring from today's values.
  • Amendment = new version, new signature, forward only. $65 → $95 applies to work after the new signature and nothing before it.
  • Unsigned means unable to claim. A contractor with no signed terms has nothing to be paid under — cleaner than any job-level gate.
  • Contract generation from settings is parked. The values are readable whenever you come to write it.
Effective dating

Commission already does this. Warranty and retention do not.

rules[].from versions commission terms by date and the rate locks at claim time. Warranty policy and retention have no dating at all today, so a change would silently apply to old defects and existing balances. That is the one place “settings are the contract” needs building rather than configuring.

Retired

  • Helping hand — they run their own businesses and settle borrowing between themselves. Not Mr Sparky's problem.
  • Quoted-by / completed-by split — replaced by one field.
  • Form 001 as an input — but nothing that reads it is removed. Pre-14th jobs claimed later still need theirs, driftOnClaim re-reads forms, and warranty on an old job comes from warrantyFromForm(). Both paths live side by side permanently.
  • Which makes the 14th a soft switch, not a cutover — nothing to migrate, no deadline, and a straggler form on the 16th for a job done on the 12th still works.
  • Warranty app screen — not building it.
  • Flat-fee warranty — not building it.
  • The warranty engine itself — Mr Sparky does all repairs in-house, so resolveWarranty() and its two-entry logic wire to nothing. The charge is a manual adjustment.
  • Category filtering — not needed. Only 003-pool jobs reach Telegram, so only they can ever be claimed or completed by a network tech. The attribution is the filter.
